Selected bibliography

Works of reason & shadow

A guided shelf of poems, tales, detective fiction, and criticism. Dates refer to first publication, with known title changes noted.

01

Poem · 1845

The Raven

A bereaved scholar receives a midnight visitor whose single answer turns recollection into ritual. First published in the Evening Mirror on January 29, 1845.

Themes: mourning, memory, obsession
02

Tale · 1843

The Tell-Tale Heart

An unnamed narrator attempts to prove perfect sanity while recounting a murder with unnerving precision. The tale first appeared in the Boston magazine The Pioneer.

Themes: guilt, perception, unreliability
03

Tale · 1839

The Fall of the House of Usher

A visitor to the Usher estate witnesses the intertwined decline of a family, its last heirs, and the house itself. First printed in Burton’s Gentleman’s Magazine.

Themes: inheritance, enclosure, decay
04

Tale · 1843

The Black Cat

A condemned narrator describes cruelty, violence, and the discovery that undoes his effort to conceal a crime. First published in the Saturday Evening Post.

Themes: conscience, perversity, confession
05

Mystery · 1841

The Murders in the Rue Morgue

C. Auguste Dupin solves an apparently impossible double murder through observation and “ratiocination,” establishing a pattern later detective fiction would follow.

Themes: deduction, urban mystery, reason
06

Mystery · 1844

The Purloined Letter

Dupin returns to recover a compromising letter hidden not through secrecy but through calculated obviousness. It first appeared in the annual The Gift.

Themes: concealment, intellect, power
07

Tale · 1842

The Masque of the Red Death

Prince Prospero seals himself and his courtiers inside an abbey, only to learn that privilege offers no refuge from mortality.

Originally published as “The Mask of the Red Death”
08

Poem · 1849

Annabel Lee

Poe’s final completed poem transforms lost love into a ballad of devotion that outlasts death. It was published after his death in 1849.

Themes: love, loss, remembrance
09

Novel · 1838

The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym

Poe’s only completed novel follows a stowaway through mutiny, shipwreck, cannibalism, and an increasingly enigmatic voyage south.

Form: maritime adventure and speculative fiction
10

Poem · 1849

The Bells

A four-part orchestration of sound moves from youthful celebration to alarm, mourning, and terror. Multiple versions survive; it appeared posthumously.

Themes: sound, time, transformation
