The life behind the work

A restless literary life

From Boston to Richmond, Baltimore, Philadelphia, and New York, Poe pursued a precarious career in the young American magazine world.

Edgar Poe was born in Boston on January 19, 1809, to the actors David and Eliza Poe. Orphaned before his third birthday, he was taken into the Richmond household of John and Frances Allan, though never formally adopted.

Education and first books

After years in Britain and Richmond, Poe entered the University of Virginia in 1826 but left amid financial conflict with John Allan. In Boston the following year, he enlisted in the U.S. Army under the name Edgar A. Perry and anonymously issued his first book, Tamerlane and Other Poems. A second collection followed in 1829. He entered West Point in 1830 and was dismissed in 1831 after deliberately neglecting his duties.

Editor, critic, and craftsman

Poe settled for a time in Baltimore with his aunt Maria Clemm and her daughter Virginia. His story “MS. Found in a Bottle” won a Baltimore literary prize in 1833, helping him secure editorial work at the Southern Literary Messenger in Richmond. He married Virginia Clemm in 1836.

Over the next decade he contributed to and edited several important magazines, including Burton’s Gentleman’s Magazine, Graham’s Magazine, and the Broadway Journal. His reviews could be severe, but his criticism argued consistently for precision, unity of effect, and high standards in American letters.

Recognition and final years

“The Raven,” published in January 1845, made Poe nationally famous, though fame did not bring lasting financial security. Virginia died of tuberculosis in January 1847. Poe continued to lecture and write, publishing the cosmological prose poem Eureka in 1848. He died in Baltimore on October 7, 1849, at age forty; the precise cause of his death remains uncertain.

Chronology

A life in dates

1809

Born in Boston

Edgar Poe is born on January 19 to professional actors Elizabeth Arnold Poe and David Poe Jr.

1811

Taken in by the Allans

After his mother’s death, Poe joins the Richmond household of John and Frances Allan.

1827

Soldier and first-time author

He enlists in the Army and publishes Tamerlane and Other Poems in Boston.

1833

A prize-winning story

“MS. Found in a Bottle” wins a fifty-dollar prize from the Baltimore Saturday Visiter.

1835

The magazine career begins

Poe joins the Southern Literary Messenger in Richmond and builds a reputation as critic and editor.

1841

Dupin enters the locked room

“The Murders in the Rue Morgue,” widely regarded as the first modern detective story, appears in Graham’s Magazine.

1845

“The Raven” brings fame

The poem appears in New York’s Evening Mirror on January 29 and is rapidly reprinted.

1849

Death in Baltimore

Poe dies on October 7. “Annabel Lee” and “The Bells” are published posthumously.
